How it works

The data

Everything here is the realm-domesday realm, stored in this world's graph: the Hull Domesday Project's database (Professor J.J.N. Palmer and team, University of Hull; CC BY-NC-SA), converted from the Domesday Explorer's 2002 tables. It does not change, so there is no refresh to wait for.

  • DomesdayPlace — 14,776 places, 13,486 of them located from their OS grid reference.
  • DomesdayEntry — the 18,174 entries of Great Domesday, each with the figures it states.
  • DomesdayPerson — 14,042 names, one record per name per county, as Hull indexes them.
  • DomesdayHundred, DomesdayCounty — the hundreds and the 34 counties.

Who held what is an edge from the entry, by role: LORD_1066, TENANT_IN_CHIEF (held of the king in 1086), TENANT_1086 (held of a tenant-in-chief), and a dozen rarer roles — Hull's own coding.

The figures were parsed from Hull's coding of the Phillimore text. Measured against Open Domesday's figures on 450 manors, they agree to 99% for villagers and smallholders, 98% for mills, meadow and slaves, 94% and 91% for value in 1066 and 1086, 88% for the men's plough teams, 84% for tax and 81% for the lord's plough teams. Every entry shows its Phillimore reference and links to its page in Open Domesday, so any figure can be checked.

The questions

The page asks the world by view name; each view is named Cypher saved in the realm.

  • On load, together: DomesdayCounties (the county list), DomesdayMapPlaces {county} (every located place with its households, value and waste) and DomesdayCountySummary (the "England in 1086" totals beside the map, summed from the same rows the Counties tab shows).
  • Search: DomesdayPlaceSearch {name, limit} and DomesdayPersonSearch {name, limit}, together, as you type.
  • A place: DomesdayPlaceEntries {placeId}; an entry in full: DomesdayEntryDetail {entryId}; nearby: DomesdayPlacesNear {lat, lng, km}.
  • A person: DomesdayPersonSummary {name} and DomesdayPersonHoldings {name}.
  • A county: DomesdayCountyFigures {county}, DomesdayHundredSummary {county}, DomesdayRichestManors {county, limit}, DomesdayWaste {county, limit}; the table: DomesdayCountySummary.
  • The Conquest: DomesdayGreatestLords1066, DomesdayGreatestLandholders, DomesdayConquestTransfers, DomesdayWomenLandholders.
  • Wealth and waste: DomesdayWaste, DomesdayRichestManors, DomesdayValueChange {direction}, DomesdayFeature {feature}.
  • Ask: gateway.kg.ask({question, explain: true}), with the question prefixed "In Domesday Book (1086): " — this world also holds modern places and census figures, and the box means 1086. The appliance's model writes Cypher over the Domesday schema, runs it, and returns the rows with the query, which this page shows.

Money is stored in pence (£1 = 20s = 240d) and shown in pounds, shillings and pence. Households are heads of household, not people.

When it looks wrong

  • A place with no figures is usually a dependency named inside another manor's entry: open its "named in" entry.
  • Essex, Norfolk and Suffolk show places but no entries — Little Domesday is not in this edition. That is a gap in the source, not "nothing there".
  • A figure missing from an entry means the entry does not state it; it is not zero.
  • One person across England is several records (one per county); the person page gathers them by name, and says how many it gathered.
  • An answer to a typed question is a query a model wrote. Read the query under it; if it counts the wrong thing, use the views on the other tabs, which are fixed.

What made this possible

  • The page has no backend of its own: it calls the world's saved views by name at load, and the appliance runs them over the graph.
  • "Who replaced whom" is one traversal — entry to its 1066 lord, entry to its 1086 tenant-in-chief — not a join written for this page.
  • The figures came out of a 2002 database whose text store was undocumented; the realm ships the converter and a test that reconciles its figures against Open Domesday.
