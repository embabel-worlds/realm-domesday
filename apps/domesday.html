Studies

The questions a historian brings to Domesday, answered across the whole survey. Each says what it counted; several are conclusions the realm's rules draw from the entries — who succeeded whom, whose man a tenant was, how far the devastation reached.

How far the devastation reached

Hundreds where much of the land lay waste, and the neighbouring hundreds the waste spread into — traced outward, hundred by hundred, from the worst. Share = waste entries ÷ entries in the hundred.

Who held the land, 1086

Each county's recorded value by holder. The bishops of Bayeux, Coutances and Lisieux held their English land as barons, not for their churches, so they are shown apart.

Who succeeded whom

A Norman tenant-in-chief and the English lord whose land he was given — Domesday's antecessor — where he holds at least half of that lord's entries in a county.

A lord's men

Sub-tenants who held two or more entries of the same tenant-in-chief in a county: the honours and household knights of 1086.

Who held on through the Conquest

Lords of 1066 still holding in 1086 under the same name in the same county. Mostly English; Domesday records no nationality, so Edward the Confessor's Normans count too. A bare name may be two men.

How scattered were the great estates

How it works

The data

Everything here is the realm-domesday realm, stored in this world's graph:

  • The figures and people: the Hull Domesday Project's database (Professor J.J.N. Palmer and team, University of Hull; CC BY-NC-SA), converted from the Domesday Explorer's tables. DomesdayEntry — the 18,174 entries of Great Domesday and the 4,397 of Little Domesday (Essex, Norfolk, Suffolk), whose figures are read from the translation; DomesdayPlace — 14,334 places, 13,068 located; DomesdayPerson — 14,042 names, one per name per county; DomesdayHundred, DomesdayCounty.
  • The text: Hull's transcription of the Phillimore translation (CC BY-NC-SA 2.0 UK), on every entry but Yorkshire's, which Hull leaves out. An entry shows the reading text; "Hull's edition" shows the apparatus — [= =] fief heading, ?M? marginalia, <210c> folio, [* *] an identification, ^[ ]^ implied meaning, { } Latin, {{ }} struck out.
  • Today: each place's parish and neighbourhood now — ModernParish (Census 2021) and ModernNeighbourhood (Indices of Deprivation 2025), from ONS and MHCLG under the Open Government Licence — found by testing the centre of its grid square against today's boundaries.
  • Links: Wikidata ids for 8,913 places and 168 great landholders (matched by hand), and through them Wikipedia, Vision of Britain and the Manorial Documents Register. Wikipedia text is fetched when you open a page, shown with its link (CC BY-SA), and not stored.

Who held what is an edge from the entry, by role: LORD_1066, TENANT_IN_CHIEF (held of the king in 1086), TENANT_1086 (held of a tenant-in-chief), and a dozen rarer roles — Hull's own coding.

The figures were parsed from Hull's coding of the Phillimore text. Measured against Open Domesday's figures on 450 manors, they agree to 99% for villagers and smallholders, 98% for mills, meadow and slaves, 94% and 91% for value in 1066 and 1086, 88% for the men's plough teams, 84% for tax and 81% for the lord's plough teams. Every entry shows its Phillimore reference and links to its folio in Open Domesday, so any figure can be checked.

The rules

Some answers are conclusions the realm draws from the entries by rule, each with its threshold stated:

  • Who succeeded whom: a tenant-in-chief holding at least half a 1066 lord's entries in a county.
  • A lord's men: a sub-tenant holding two or more entries of one tenant-in-chief.
  • Devastation: a hundred 40% waste, or 15% beside a devastated hundred, traced outward.
  • Held on through the Conquest: the same name holding in 1066 and 1086 in the same county.

The questions

The page asks the world by view name; each view is named Cypher saved in the realm.

  • On load: DomesdayCounties, DomesdayMapPlaces {county}, DomesdayCountySummary. Search: DomesdayPlaceSearch, DomesdayPersonSearch.
  • A place: DomesdayPlaceEntries {placeId}, DomesdayThenAndNow {placeId}, DomesdayPlaceWikipedia {placeId}, and on request DomesdayPlaceToday {placeId} (live district figures from realm-uk-streets, when installed) and DomesdayPlacesNear. An entry: DomesdayEntryDetail {entryId}.
  • A person: DomesdayPersonSummary, DomesdayPersonHoldings, DomesdayPersonWikipedia, DomesdayAntecessors, DomesdayMenOf; on request DomesdayWikipediaMentions and DomesdayWhatItSaysAbout.
  • A county: DomesdayCountyFigures, DomesdayHundredSummary, DomesdayRichestManors {county, kind, limit}, DomesdayWaste.
  • The Conquest and Wealth tabs: DomesdayGreatestLords1066, DomesdayGreatestLandholders, DomesdayConquestTransfers, DomesdayWomenLandholders, DomesdayValueChange, DomesdayFeature.
  • Studies: DomesdayDevastation, DomesdayWhoHeldTheLand, DomesdayAntecessors, DomesdayMenOf, DomesdayConquestSurvivors, DomesdayFateOfEnglishLords, DomesdayValueAnomalies (a least-squares fit of value on resources), DomesdayWomenBeforeAfter, DomesdayPloughStocking, DomesdayGrowth, DomesdayLostVillages, DomesdayRichThenRichNow (a correlation), DomesdayFeeScatter.
  • The text: DomesdayTextMentions and DomesdayTextMentionsByCounty (exact, complete); DomesdaySearchText (a judged search over the chapter documents); DomesdayExtractFromText.
  • Ask: gateway.kg.ask({question, explain: true}), with the question prefixed "In Domesday Book (1086): ". The appliance's model writes Cypher over the Domesday schema, runs it, and returns the rows with the query, which this page shows.

Money is stored in pence (£1 = 20s = 240d) and shown in pounds, shillings and pence. Households are heads of household, not people.

When it looks wrong

  • A place with no figures is usually a dependency named inside another manor's entry: open its "named in" entry.
  • Essex, Norfolk and Suffolk are Little Domesday: their figures are read from the translation by a parser (85–100% agreement with Hull's hand-coded figures on most fields, 76–84% for plough teams), not from Hull's coding. Yorkshire has figures but no text.
  • A figure missing from an entry means the entry does not state it; it is not zero.
  • One person across England is several records (one per county); the person page gathers them by name, and a common name may be several men.
  • "Then and now" tests one point, the centre of a 1 km square: a vill near a parish boundary can land in its neighbour, and "possibly lost" is a candidate, not a finding.
  • An account of a person, a search by meaning and a list pulled out of the text are written by the appliance's model from the entries shown with them. Read the entries.
  • An answer to a typed question is a query a model wrote. Read the query under it; if it counts the wrong thing, use the views on the other tabs, which are fixed.

What made this possible

  • The page has no backend of its own: it calls the world's saved views by name, and the appliance runs them over the graph.
  • "Who succeeded whom" and "how far the devastation reached" are rules the realm declares; the appliance evaluates them to a fixpoint when a view asks, so no table of conclusions was ever computed or stored.
  • A place's figures for today come from another realm, joined on the ONS codes both share — no copy of that data is held here.
  • The figures came out of a 2002 database whose text store was undocumented; the realm ships the converter and a test that reconciles its figures against Open Domesday.
